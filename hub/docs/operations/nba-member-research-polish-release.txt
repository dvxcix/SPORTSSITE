Member interface
NBA headshots use a closer face crop inside the existing clipped team-gradient
frames across Court research, player profiles and shared sports avatars.
Position labels and matrix indicators remain outside the image crop.
Sportsbook filters show a stack of actual available sportsbook logos; selecting
one book shows its logo. First Basket uses the same accessible selector.
Two-team selectors show both actual team logos.
Normal member refresh controls are removed. Automatic updates remain;
error-only Retry controls recover failed shared reads and cannot force an
upstream refresh. No tooltips or explanatory panels were added.

Shared loading and freshness
Public sports inputs are cached through database leases across instances,
with request coalescing and a bounded 32 MiB / 256-entry process cache.
Failure cooldowns prevent repeated callers from retrying a failing source.
Cold followers use bounded backoff. Exact-price validation uses short-lived
shared public inputs while validating every submitted selection independently.
Member identities, saved picks, ownership and matrix results are not shared.

Regular odds use 30-second source caches; First Basket uses 60 seconds.
Pick validation uses 15-second public inputs and still rejects changed prices,
wrong contracts and games that have started. Research fallback cannot carry
expired player or game prices into a new board. Quotes are attached after
research resolves. Assembled Slate Edge results have a further 30-second cache;
their price inputs are rechecked after the slate research has loaded.
Public activity, artwork, player identity and historical opening-play research
also share their public inputs. Partial First Basket research retries on the
normal short cadence rather than remaining cached for an hour.

Visible tools poll automatically with jitter and a full cadence between focus
updates. Hidden views are quiet and pending requests are not duplicated.
Accounts without enabled NBA matrices perform one check for the slate instead
of one per game, then use a five-minute cadence. Matrix edits still trigger an
immediate evaluation. Slow matrix evaluations can finish before the next poll.
Member board routes allow up to 120 seconds for cold shared work; existing NFL
timeouts and collectors are unchanged.
